# How the cable-driven rear handbrake could be modeled better (research note, no code change)

Date: 2026-10-15 (PT). The handbrake stays as it is for now: `max(handbrakeFraction x 1500 N m, hydraulic rear torque)`, an instant, linear torque of 1500 N m per rear wheel (an estimate, E) that locks the rear wheels at any lever position above about 20 %. This note collects what a better model would contain. Tags as in `docs/BrakeSources.md`.

## What the real mechanism is
- Hand lever (ratchet) -> front cable -> equalizer (a pivoting lever or U-shaped guide that gives both rear cables the same tension and carries the adjuster) -> two rear cables, each through a sheath into the rear brake backing plate -> the parking lever hinged at the top of the trailing shoe web, which pushes the leading shoe through a strut. For the Scirocco II the rear brake is the 180 mm self-adjusting drum; the layout is the usual integrated drum parking brake (general layout S: Ford brake text https://www.ford-taurus.org/taurusinfo/Features/ParkBrake.pdf; the Scirocco-specific lever geometry is not published: E).
- The force chain is: hand force x lever ratio x cable efficiency x (equalizer ratio) x drum lever ratio = shoe apply force, then torque = drum brake factor x shoe apply force x drum radius (the same shoe pair as the foot brake, so the sim's rear drum model from the experimental brakes can be reused and no second torque constant is needed).

## Proposed model, from simplest to most detailed
1. **Force path instead of a fixed torque (small step).** Handbrake input -> hand force (a lever with a load cell does not exist, so use lever position) -> shoe force through a ratio `handbrakeCableToShoeForceRatio` (E, to be bounded from the requirement below) -> `DrumBrakeModel` torque. Keeps the max-with-hydraulic rule (the same shoes, so the larger of the two actuation forces is the physically right combination only if both act on the same lever; a sum of forces on the shoe pair is closer to reality and should replace the max).
2. **Travel and slack.** Lever ratchet clicks to the first brake contact (shoe clearance after the adjuster, E), cable stretch as a compliance (S for the existence, E for the value): travel = free travel + cable compliance x tension. This gives the familiar "pull until the rears bite, then a firm lever".
3. **Cable friction and hysteresis.** Force at the shoe lever lags the lever force by Coulomb friction in the sheath: tension loss exp(-mu x total bend angle) per bend (published form for Bowden cables, https://engineering.purdue.edu/~byao/Papers/TRO_Modeling_2010.pdf), with different efficiency on apply and release (hysteresis: the handbrake releases late) and dead zones from static friction. Measured force and travel efficiencies of real parking brake cable constructions exist (Stoloff, SAE 2011-01-2380, https://doi.org/10.4271/2011-01-2380); the numbers must be pulled from the paper (not opened here) before use. Typical efficiency spread depends on conduit and strand type, so keep it a parameter (E with bounds).
4. **Equalizer and left-right difference.** The equalizer gives equal tension; a difference arises only from unequal cable friction or shoe adjustment (E). A small asymmetry parameter makes the car pull to one side on a handbrake turn.
5. **Direction and brake factor.** The foot brake is leading-trailing (same factor both directions). The strut-actuated parking lever acts on both shoes from the top, so the same brake factor pair applies; if the shoe geometry later shows a duo-servo arrangement the factor would differ by direction (check).
6. **Self-adjuster interplay.** The self-adjuster follows foot brake applications; it does not change the handbrake model except through the slack (item 2).

## Calibration anchors (sourced)
- Requirement: a hand-operated parking brake must hold the vehicle 5 minutes forward and reverse on a 20 % grade with a hand force of at most 400 N, measured at the grip center or 40 mm from the lever end (FMVSS 135 S7.12, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-571/subpart-B/section-571.135; ECE R13 similar). The older FMVSS 105 used a 30 % grade and 90 lb (about 400 N) hand force. For this car (995 kg, rear static load about 40 %, grade 20 %: rear brake torque to hold about 0.2 x 995 x 9.81 x 0.289 = 564 N m for both rears, i.e. at least about 280 N m per rear wheel, with the weight transfer to the lower axle adding a little) this is a lower bound for a real handbrake: roughly 300-600 N m per rear wheel at full lever, not 1500 N m. The current 1500 N m is therefore a stop-the-wheel-instantly estimate, several times a real parking brake; a better model would lower it and let the lever travel set the torque. The numbers in this bullet are derived (D) from the sourced requirement and the sim's mass and radius; they are not a measurement of this car.
- Design approach for lever effort, ratio and travel of rear-drum parking brakes: SAE 2015-26-0078, https://doi.org/10.4271/2015-26-0078 (abstract read only).

## What would need measuring on a real car
Lever travel to first bite and to full lever (ratchet clicks), hand force at the lever versus rear wheel drag torque (a drag meter on the rear wheel), cable stretch under a known pull, left versus right drag with the equalizer centered, and the shoe lever ratio at the backing plate.

## Recommendation
Implement items 1 to 3 together with a lower, requirement-based full-lever torque (to be agreed), as a separate experimental toggle or as part of the brake model, after the shoe geometry gives the brake factor. No code was changed for this note.
